    Duffy Solar event set in Bay City amid data center debate

    • By Kavan Van Hal | kavan.van.hal@baycitytribune.com

      kavanvanhal

    Linea Energy will host a community lunch-and-learn June 29 to discuss the Duffy Solar project planned for Matagorda County.

    The free event is scheduled for 11:30 a.m. to 1 p.m. Monday, June 29, at Schulman’s Movie Bowl, 6655 Seventh St., Suite 100, in Bay City.

    The meeting is intended to give residents a chance to meet project representatives, learn more about Linea Energy and ask questions about the planned solar development.

    Residents planning to attend are asked to RSVP by emailing Ellie Dontes, development associate for Linea Energy, at ellie.dontes@lineaenergy.com.

    Linea Energy is developing Duffy Solar as a 500-megawatt solar project in Matagorda County. The company says the project would generate enough electricity to power about 85,000 Texas homes each year once operational.

    The project is planned near Bay City and has been described as a utility-scale renewable energy development spanning about 3,500 acres. 

    Construction is expected to begin in the third quarter of 2026, with commercial operations targeted for late 2027.

    Duffy Solar is not a data center. However, the project has drawn attention locally because of its connection to Google and the growing power demand tied to data centers, cloud computing and artificial intelligence. 

    Google announced earlier this year that it signed a 15-year power purchase agreement with Linea Energy for 500 megawatts of solar energy from the Matagorda County project. The power is intended to support Google’s data center operations within the Electric Reliability Council of Texas market, commonly known as ERCOT.

    That connection places the solar project within a broader local debate about large-scale energy and technology infrastructure, even though the project itself is a power generation facility rather than a data center.

    A 235-megawatt Linea Energy battery storage facility is also under development nearby, according to published reports. The battery project should be described separately from the Google agreement unless Linea confirms it is part of the same support arrangement.

    The lunch-and-learn comes as Matagorda County residents continue discussing the effects of data centers and related infrastructure on land use, electricity demand, water resources and rural communities. 

    Residents associated with Matagorda County Against Data Centers, or MAD, have attended Commissioners Court and local economic development meetings in recent weeks. The group has also continued organizing online, with its petition reaching 792 signatures as of the latest count provided.

    A recent MAD event in Matagorda County included both outgoing Republican Texas Agriculture Commissioner Sid Miller and Clayton Tucker, the Democratic nominee for Texas agriculture commissioner. The two spoke during a June 11 forum focused on data centers and their potential effects on rural Texas. 

    The appearance of both candidates at the event highlighted how concerns about data centers and the infrastructure supporting them have crossed party lines in some rural communities. 

    Supporters of renewable energy and data infrastructure projects often point to construction activity, tax revenue, new power generation and economic development. Opponents argue those benefits should be weighed against possible effects on farmland, rural character, public infrastructure and local resources.

    Google has said the Linea agreement is part of its broader effort to add renewable energy to the Texas grid while supporting its operations.

     

    “By collaborating with Linea Energy to bring new low-cost power to the grid, we are helping to ensure the Lone Star State’s energy system remains affordable for local families and businesses,” said Will Conkling, Google’s director of energy and power.

     

    Linea Energy CEO Cassidy DeLine said the agreement reflects the company’s work with large clean-energy buyers.

     

    “We are pleased to sign this agreement to supply clean energy in Texas to Google, one of the world’s largest buyers of clean power,” DeLine said. “This transaction highlights Linea’s credibility amongst the largest hyperscalers and the ability to support affordability and grid reliability.”

     

    Texas has become a major market for both renewable energy and data center development because of its land availability, energy market, transmission infrastructure and growing electricity demand.

     

    Google has also announced other large renewable energy agreements connected to data center operations, including a 1-gigawatt solar agreement with TotalEnergies in Texas and a 1.2-gigawatt agreement with Clearway Energy involving operations in Texas, Missouri and West Virginia.

     

    Google has publicly committed to reaching net-zero emissions across its operations and value chain by 2030. The company is also pursuing what it calls its “24/7 carbon-free energy” initiative, which aims to match electricity use with carbon-free energy sources every hour of every day.

     

    The June 29 lunch-and-learn will give residents a chance to hear directly from Linea Energy representatives as questions continue about how Duffy Solar fits into Matagorda County’s energy, land use and economic future.

    Kavan Van Hal is the Managing Editor at the Bay CityTribune he can be reached at Kavan.van.hal@baycitytribune.com
